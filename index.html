AI Sales Assistant for Inbound Leads: How to Choose One

An AI sales assistant answers a visitor's question on your website, qualifies them in the same conversation, and hands sales the context. It works the traffic you already have. It does not prospect.
VP
Vijay Satya Prakash
November 5, 2026

That bot wasn't slow or rude. It answered a question its content didn't cover, and it sounded sure. Keep that in mind. It's the first test we'll ask you to run.

This guide covers what an AI sales assistant does, how to choose between them, and how to test one before you buy.

What is an AI sales assistant?

An AI sales assistant is software that talks to visitors on your website, answers their questions from your own content, works out whether they're worth a sales conversation, and passes the qualified ones to your team.

It works on people who are already on your site. It does not go out and find them.

That last sentence is the whole distinction, and most buyers miss it. The category has collected a lot of names: sales AI assistant, AI powered sales assistant, SDR agent, AI SDR tools. They get used as if they mean the same thing. They don't.

So before you compare features or pricing, answer one question: do you need something that handles the traffic you already have, or something that creates traffic you don't? Everything else follows from that.

Inbound or outbound? Decide this first

Buyers lose weeks here, so it's worth two minutes. Outbound tools build lists, write sequences and contact people who've never heard of you. Inbound tools work the traffic your marketing already paid for. Both get sold as conversational AI for sales, and the demos look similar.

The tell is where the conversation starts. If your problem is an empty pipeline and no one knows you exist, you need outbound. If your problem is that people visit, read, and leave without talking to anyone, you need inbound. Buying the wrong one is expensive, and you usually find out in month three.

This guide covers inbound only. If you're hiring an SDR agent to prospect cold accounts, the tools below are the wrong shortlist.

What an inbound assistant should do

  • Answer a question from your content while the visitor is still reading
  • Ask qualifying questions inside that conversation, not in a form afterward
  • Score the visitor so sales knows who to call first
  • Pass the conversation, not just the email address
  • Hand off to a person when it can't help
  • Offer a meeting when the visitor is ready, not on page load

Five tests to run before you buy

Demos are rehearsed. These five tests aren't, and you can run all of them in a trial. We've answered each one for ZipTier so you have a filled-in example to score other tools against. Everything below is how ZipTier is documented to work, so verify it in your own trial rather than taking our word for it.

Test 1: ask it something your content doesn't cover

The single most useful test. Load your content, then ask a question you know isn't in there. Price for a custom plan is a good one.

ZipTier's assistants answer from the files and pages you upload. When a question falls outside that, the documented behavior is to say it doesn't know rather than offer a plausible answer. On pricing specifically, it won't quote a number that isn't in the knowledge base, though it can explain the pricing model. Separately, a Knowledge Base Gaps panel collects the questions it couldn't fully answer, which is how you find out what your site is missing.

What to watch for in any tool: a confident, invented answer. Ask the same question three times and see if the answer changes.

Test 2: look at the scoring rules

Ask to see the qualification logic, not a sample score.

ZipTier grades each lead High, Medium or Low from two weighted groups. Fit comes from answers to the questions you chose. Intent comes from signals in the conversation, including BANT: budget, authority, need and timing, plus how close the visitor is to purchase. You set the weights per campaign, score each answer from 0 to 100, and set your own Medium and High cutoffs. The defaults are intent weighted at 100%, fit at 0%, with cutoffs at 40 and 70. Guardrails can cap a lead at Medium.

What to watch for: if you can't change the weights, sales will argue with the scores and eventually ignore them.

Test 3: trigger a hot lead and watch what sales gets

Run a conversation that should score high. Then look at the alert.

ZipTier waits about five minutes after a lead scores High and re-checks the tier before alerting. If it's still High, sales gets an email with the lead details, a conversation summary and the intent signals. A Book a meeting button appears for high-scoring visitors, opening Calendly or Google Calendar inside the chat.

What to watch for: an alert with a name and an email and nothing else. Your rep then opens the conversation cold, which is the problem you were trying to solve.

Test 4: ask for a human

Type something only a person can resolve.

On ZipTier's paid plans, Live Chat lets a visitor reach your team inside the same assistant. Your team replies from Slack and the reply also reaches the visitor by email. Worth being precise about the limit: this is a visitor-initiated route, not an automatic escalation the assistant decides on.

What to watch for: tools with no human path at all, and tools where the handoff drops the conversation history.

Test 5: time the setup yourself

Don't ask how long setup takes. Do it, with a stopwatch.

ZipTier uses a three-step wizard covering the campaign, the assistant's appearance and the knowledge base, with content pulled from uploaded files or your site URLs. It's documented as going live in under ten minutes, deployed as an embedded widget, a full page, a link or a QR code. Leads sync natively to HubSpot, matched on email so you don't get duplicates. Other CRMs are served by email alerts and CSV export rather than a native connector, which is a real limitation if you're on Salesforce.

What to watch for: any tool where step one is a call with an implementation consultant. That's not a bad tool, it's a different budget.

FAQs

What's the difference between an SDR and a BDR?

Both roles identify and qualify potential customers and help book sales meetings. A common distinction is that sales development reps (SDRs) handle inbound interest, such as demo requests and website inquiries, while business development reps (BDRs) focus on outbound prospecting. Companies use these titles differently, so the responsibilities matter more than the label.

An inbound AI sales assistant automates parts of inbound sales development, such as answering visitor questions, qualifying leads, and booking meetings, rather than outbound prospecting.

Is an AI sales assistant the same as an AI SDR?

Not quite, and the difference matters when you're buying. AI SDR usually describes tools that run outbound sequences at scale. An inbound AI sales assistant works on people already on your site. Some vendors use both terms for the same product, so ask directly whether it prospects or responds.

What does an AI sales assistant cost?

AI sales assistant software is priced in three different shapes, and the shape matters more than the brand. Per-seat tools like Intercom run $19 to $132 per seat per month, with AI usage billed on top. Conversation-based tools like ZipTier run from $99 to $1,999 a month. Enterprise platforms like Qualified don't publish pricing at all. Figures checked November 2, 2026.

Can I set one up without a developer?

For most inbound tools, yes. Setup is usually uploading content, choosing your qualifying questions and pasting a snippet into your site. ZipTier documents a three-step wizard and a live assistant in under ten minutes. Enterprise platforms are different and often involve an implementation project.

Does it work with HubSpot?

ZipTier syncs leads to HubSpot natively, matching on email so you don't create duplicate contacts. Other CRMs are supported through email alerts and CSV export rather than a native connector. If you're on Salesforce and need direct write-back, ask every vendor on your shortlist about it specifically.

What happens when it can't answer a question?

This varies more than any other behavior, which is why it's test one above. Some tools generate a plausible answer. Better ones say they don't know and offer a person. ZipTier is documented to say it doesn't know rather than guess, and it logs the gap so you can add the missing content.

What's the best AI sales assistant for a B2B website?

It depends on whether you need support deflection or sales qualification, which is criterion 9 above. A support-first tool and a sales-first tool can look identical in a demo and behave differently in month two. For a ranked comparison of the main tools, including which have free tiers and how long setup takes, see our guide to the best AI chatbot for website lead capture.

Can an AI sales assistant book meetings?

Most can. ZipTier shows a Book a meeting button once a visitor scores High, and the scheduler, either Calendly or Google Calendar, opens inside the chat rather than sending the visitor to another page. Ask any vendor whether booking happens in the conversation or after a redirect. The drop-off between those two is real.

How is an AI sales assistant different from live chat?

Live chat routes a visitor to a person, so the answer depends on someone being available. An AI sales assistant answers immediately from your content at any hour, then brings in a person when it can't help. Most teams run both, with the assistant handling the first question and live chat handling what it can't.

Do I still need lead forms?

Usually yes, for the people who prefer them. The difference is what each one does. A form collects details and answers nothing. A conversation answers the question first, then collects details once the visitor has a reason to give them. Keep the form, stop making it the only way through.

Will it annoy my visitors?

It will if it opens with a pop-up demand before the page has finished loading. Visitors object to interruption, not to help. Judge a tool on when it speaks and what it says first, not on whether it speaks at all.

How long before I see results?

Conversations start in week one, and the knowledge gaps show up immediately, which is useful on its own. Qualified leads depend on your traffic volume. Sales-accepted leads are the slowest signal and usually take a quarter. Be wary of any vendor who quotes you a timeline without asking about your traffic first.

In a nutshell

If you take one thing from this: run test one on every tool you shortlist. Ask it something your content doesn't cover and watch what it does. That single question separates tools you can put in front of a prospect from tools you can't.
